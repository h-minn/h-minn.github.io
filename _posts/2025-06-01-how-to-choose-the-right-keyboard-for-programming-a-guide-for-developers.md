## 🔍 Why Keyboard Choice Matters for Programmers

As developers, we type a **lot**. From writing code to documentation, shell commands to commit messages — we’re constantly on the keys. Choosing the **right keyboard** can:

- Reduce fatigue and strain
- Improve typing accuracy and speed
- Make long coding sessions more enjoyable

Let’s break down how to pick one that fits **your style** and **workflow**.

---

## 📏 Keyboard Sizes: Finding the Right Fit

Choosing a keyboard size is about **space, usage, and habit**. Here are the most common layouts:

### 🖥️ Full Size (100%)
- Includes everything: alphanumeric keys, function row, arrow keys, and a numpad
- Great for Excel or finance-related work
- Takes up a lot of desk space

### 🖥️ Tenkeyless (TKL - 87%)
- Drops the numpad for a more compact layout
- Keeps arrow keys and function row
- A solid choice for most programmers

### 💻 75% Layout
- A tighter TKL layout
- Keys are closer together but nothing essential is removed
- Excellent balance between size and functionality

### 🧩 65% and 60%
- Removes the function row and/or arrow keys (depending on layout)
- Ultra-compact — great for portability
- May require custom key mapping or function layers

---

## 🔄 Switch Types: Feel Matters

When it comes to **mechanical switches**, it’s all about **feel and sound**. The three main types:

### 🔵 Clicky (e.g., Blue Switches)
- **Tactile bump** + **loud click**
- Feels satisfying, like a typewriter
- Great for people who enjoy audio feedback
- Can be loud for shared workspaces

### 🟢 Tactile (e.g., Brown Switches)
- Tactile bump without the click
- Balanced and quiet
- Ideal for mixed-use environments

### 🔴 Linear (e.g., Red Switches)
- Smooth press, no tactile bump or click
- Light and quiet
- Popular with gamers or fast typists

## 🎯 Final Thoughts

Choosing the right keyboard is a personal journey — it depends on your workspace, typing preferences, and aesthetic taste.

If you’re:
- New to mechanical keyboards, try a **hot-swappable board** so you can test different switches
- A developer who values feedback and fun, consider **blue switches**
- Short on desk space but still want function keys, go for a **65% or 75% layout**

Whatever you choose, remember: your keyboard should support your **productivity**, not fight it.
